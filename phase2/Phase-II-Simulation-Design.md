## 1. Purpose and scope

Phase II tests whether the CCA instrument detects clinically meaningful deviations earlier than conventional monitoring, at a matched false-alarm rate, in controlled simulations where the truth is known. It addresses research hypotheses RH1 to RH4. RH5, the effect of predefined interventions, belongs to Phase III, and section 12 only defines the interface.

**What Phase II can show.** Detection performance, lead time, false-alarm behavior, attribution (which indicators respond to which mechanism), and the bias of the EHOR estimator, all under stated assumptions.

**What it cannot show.** Prospective clinical benefit, the realism of any injected drift, how real reviewers behave, or how often the modeled failures occur in practice. It is retrospective simulation, and reports must say so.

**Scope limit.** The anchor is predictive AI. Of the 41 indicators, 34 carry the `predictive` tag and are exercised here. G1 to G6 and L2 (generative and agentic only) are outside Phase II and need a separate study.

## 3. Data and stream construction

**Tier 1, synthetic.** A latent generator draws features, a subgroup attribute with a configurable share, and outcomes from a known model. Case severity is drawn from an ordinal model so that missed cases in high-risk strata are more severe. The deployed model is fit on a separate training sample (proposed 20,000 cases, disjoint from every window). The 52 baseline windows are used only to set VOE baselines. Fitting and baselining on the same cases would make baseline performance optimistic and inflate false alarms. Every perturbation in the catalog is a defined operation on the generator (section 5), so onset, magnitude, and every held-stable property are known exactly.

**Tier 2, semi-synthetic on MIMIC-IV or eICU.** The dataset references are MIMIC-IV [johnson2023] and the eICU Collaborative Research Database [pollard2018]. The features and labels come from real records, with perturbations applied to them (importance resampling for covariate shift, injected missingness, rescaling, relabeling for concept drift). Replicate streams are formed by block bootstrap over windows [kunsch1989].

Two points need confirming against the dataset documentation before Tier 2 is designed in detail. First, MIMIC-IV dates are, as I understand it, shifted per patient, with only a coarse calendar grouping available. If so, cases cannot be ordered by true calendar time across patients. The project lead's prior work reports a temporal holdout in MIMIC-IV [yu2026deferral], so ordering at calendar-group level appears usable, and the exact grain is still to be confirmed. Finer streams would be constructed in a randomized order within calendar groups, and natural drift could be explored only at group level. Injected perturbations would supply the temporal structure. Second, replicate streams are resamples of one dataset. They are not independent draws from a patient population, so Monte Carlo intervals describe simulation error conditional on that dataset, not variation across hospitals or years.

eICU is a multi-hospital ICU dataset. As I understand it, its records carry a hospital identifier and time offsets relative to ICU admission, not calendar dates. If so, it would allow a natural between-site shift (fit on some hospitals, monitor a stream drawn from others), which no injected perturbation can imitate, and it could serve as an external source for a model developed on MIMIC-IV. It would not support calendar-ordered streams. Both points are to be confirmed against its documentation. Any scenario built from natural shift needs its expected responders written into the catalog, as a dated amendment, before it is run.

ICU missingness can itself carry clinical information [yu2026missingness], so any missingness injected in Tier 2 must state its mechanism (random, as in SC05, or informative, as in SC06) and how the deployed model handles missing values.

Reviewer and workflow layers stay simulated in both tiers.

**Data access and governance.** Record the version and modules used of each dataset. Confirm the terms of each data use agreement before any AI-assisted work touches the data, since agreements for credentialed datasets commonly restrict sharing with third parties, and a hosted AI service may count as one. Proposed rule: row-level data stays on your machine and is never pasted into an AI session. Code is developed and tested against a schema-matched synthetic stand-in, and only schemas and aggregate outputs are shared.

**Configurations and factors.** The primary configuration is 500 cases per window, label lag of 2 windows, and a subgroup share of 10 percent. Sensitivity varies one factor at a time: cases per window (100 and 2,000), label lag (0 and 8 windows), and subgroup share (30 percent, for the two subgroup scenarios only). Label lag matters because outcome-based indicators cannot alert before their labels arrive, while label-free indicators can. The catalog field `label_dependence` records whether the primary responders of a scenario need labels (`label_free`, `label_dependent`, or `none` for the control).

## 4. Ground truth

### 4.1 Deviation start and onset

Two times are distinguished.

- The **deviation start** s0 is the window in which the perturbation begins. For an abrupt shape it is the injection window. For a gradual or incremental shape it is the start of the ramp.
- The **onset** t0 is the first window in which the onset ratio reaches (1 + kappa) (decision D18). The onset ratio is the larger of the pooled expected residual harm exposure (section 4.3) relative to its baseline and the same ratio for a predefined subgroup, each relative to its own baseline, computed from the generator's truth and not from realized events. A subgroup counts only if it is at least 5 percent of cases, and the VOE fixes a short list of such subgroups (about three attributes), so that onset does not depend on a noisy small stratum. Always t0 is at or after s0.

Defining onset by s0 would credit a monitor with a delay it could never have avoided, because a ramp has not yet become clinically meaningful. A **sub-threshold cell** is one in which expected RHE never reaches the threshold within the monitored period. It has no onset. It is reported by alert rate (the share of replicates with an alert episode starting at or after s0), excluded from every TAFD test, and counted. Milestone M2 computes the expected-RHE trajectory of every cell and lists sub-threshold cells for each kappa in {10, 25, 50} percent.

### 4.2 Alerts, detection, and false alarms

An **alert episode** starts when system status rises from green to the analyzed level or higher and ends after j consecutive green windows (the hysteresis of `Instrument-Specification.md` section 4.2). The analyzed level is yellow, and orange is the secondary "actionable" level. For each episode start at window u in a perturbed stream:

| Episode start | Classification | TAFD |
|---|---|---|
| u before s0 | False alarm | not applicable |
| u from s0 up to (not including) t0 | Anticipatory detection: the monitor warned before the deviation became meaningful | 0, with lead time t0 - u recorded |
| u at or after t0 | Detection | u - t0 |

An episode that began before s0 and is still active at s0 is a false-alarm episode and does not count as a detection. Detection then requires a new episode or, for the orange analysis, an escalation. The number of replicates in which a pre-s0 episode is active at s0 is reported, and the analysis is repeated without them. A replicate with no qualifying alert is right-censored. The common horizon is h = 40 windows after onset. For abrupt shapes follow-up is at least 45 windows (104 - 60 + 1), so it is complete. For ramped shapes s0 is drawn from windows 20 to 40, and any replicate whose follow-up after t0 is shorter than h is administratively censored and handled by the Kaplan-Meier estimate.

### 4.3 Residual harm exposure

To define delta the same way for model, reviewer, and workflow scenarios, define the residual harm exposure per 1,000 cases,

`RHE = 1000 * (1/N) * sum_i w(sev_i) * E_i * (1 - I_i)`

where E_i = 1 if case i is either

- an AI error of any severity, a false positive or a false negative, or
- a time-critical case whose output reaches the point of care after its clinical window (delay-attributable harm, with a severity assigned per case),

w(sev) is the severity weight (1, 5, and 50 for classes 1 to 3, times 3 for a missed deterioration; decision D3), and I_i = 1 if a reviewer intercepted the error before the point of no return. For delay-attributable harm, I_i = 0. A meaningful deviation is expected RHE of at least (1 + kappa) times its baseline value. RHE rises when the model degrades, when reviewers miss more errors, when workflow faults let errors through, and when delay affects time-critical cases. It comes from the simulation's hidden truth and is never visible to the monitors. RHE is residual after reviewer interception. It is distinct from the plan's secondary outcome "residual error or risk after intervention", which concerns governance interventions and will be measured in Phase III, using RHE after the governance actions.

The severity cutoff s* is not used in RHE. EHOR uses it to define which errors require human detection.

Scenarios that have no patient effect by design (SC12, SC15, SC18, SC21, SC23, SC24), harmless-drift scenarios whose harm never reaches (1 + kappa) times baseline RHE (SC01, SC05, SC06), and SC09, which has no pooled onset at a 10 percent subgroup share, are marked `primary_tafd: false` in the catalog and are excluded from the primary TAFD analysis. The threshold rule was fixed before any monitor ran. Their detection delay from the fault time (the s0 of that scenario) is reported separately as a process-detection delay.

## 5. Scenario catalog

`scenarios.yaml` holds 25 scenarios: one control (SC00) and 24 perturbations. Each has three magnitude levels and one to three shapes, giving 105 scenario-level-shape cells in the primary configuration. Every scenario records, before any run:

- `primary`: indicators that must respond by mechanism.
- `secondary`: indicators expected to respond downstream.
- `held_stable`: indicators constant by construction (a generator constraint checked in tests).
- `expected_null`: indicators the mechanism does not touch, used as negative controls.

Examples of what this buys. SC02 (prevalence shift at fixed class-conditional distributions) holds Se, Sp, and AUROC invariant in the population by construction while calibration-in-the-large and predictive values move. SC08 and SC17 are fair tests in which calibration and performance move together, one by changing the world and the other by regressing the model. SC25 changes two things at once. SC23 changes only the displayed confidence, so ranking, decisions, sensitivity, and specificity are exactly unchanged. SC04 is a deliberate boundary case: discrimination falls at maintained calibration, so calibration indicators should not lead. SC08 leaves inputs unchanged, so input-shift indicators should stay silent. SC10 changes only reviewer behavior, so model-level monitoring cannot detect it by construction.

Each scenario also has a `purpose`. A `fair_test` is one in which conventional metrics can respond and there is a harm-defined onset. A `blind_spot` is one in which conventional metrics are blind by construction. `harmless_drift` is drift with no meaningful harm, used to measure false-positive governance alerts. `process` covers rule-based and governance-record deviations. Levels of fair-test and blind-spot scenarios are defined on the harm scale: the catalog records `harm_targets` (ratios of expected RHE to baseline), and a test checks that the levels still reproduce them, so a change to the harm weights is noticed.

`scripts/validate_scenarios.py` enforces that all referenced indicators exist and apply to predictive AI, that the response sets do not contradict each other, that all 34 predictive-applicable indicators are a primary responder in at least one scenario, that each of RH1 to RH4 has at least two scenarios, and that each of RH1 and RH2 has at least three fair-test scenarios with a harm-defined onset. It was tested against eight deliberate corruptions. The expectations are fixed before running and change only by a dated amendment.

## 6. Systems compared

**Conventional monitor.** P1, P2, and P4 tracked with the same detector family as CCA. This is the plan's comparator: monitoring based primarily on model-performance metrics.

**CCA monitor.** All 34 predictive-applicable indicators, each with the detection method in its record, combined by the routing rule of `Instrument-Specification.md` section 4.2. The primary configuration is persistence k = 2 with corroboration on (D7), which applies to orange and red status and is not implemented, so every result so far is yellow-level (section 16.11). The other combinations (k of 1 or 3, corroboration off) are sensitivity analyses. Each is calibrated separately to the same false-alarm budget and none is selected on performance.

At the yellow level, the primary rule (decision D13) is a **component-level budget split**: each state component (C, P, U, and so on) receives an equal share of the false-alarm budget and is calibrated on its own, with one common multiplier so that the union of components meets the system budget. It was chosen for interpretability, because an alert then names a component and its owner, and not because it is more sensitive. Measured on null streams, an equal split lowers the performance component's threshold only marginally against the union rule. The union-of-all-indicators rule is a required sensitivity analysis, and a tilted split (60 percent to performance) is reported for information only and is never selected.

**Ablation arms** attribute any gain to a domain without depending on the order in which domains are added. Add-one arms are A0 plus U, A0 plus C, A0 plus H, A0 plus O, and A0 plus L, where A0 is the conventional monitor. Leave-one-out arms are full CCA minus each of U, C, H, O, and L. Here U is calibration and uncertainty, C context and data, H oversight, O operational, and L lifecycle.

**Observables.** Each indicator needs simulator outputs that must exist. For example, L4 needs change records, L3 needs trace logs, C3 needs invocation scope flags, O6 needs safety events, and H-indicators need review logs. Milestone M0 maps every `data_required` element of every predictive-applicable indicator to a simulator output. An indicator with an unmapped element is dropped from the CCA arm and listed as untested.

**Detector families** (implementation defaults, configurable). Binomial proportions (P1, P2, P3, U4, most H and O indicators) use sequential probability ratio tests [wald1945] or CUSUM [page1954]. Continuous quantities (U1 to U3, P4) use EWMA [roberts1959] with bootstrap confidence bounds. Distribution indicators (C1, C2, U5) use windowed two-sample tests, with kernel discrepancy [gretton2012] or a distance measure, and false-discovery-rate control [benjamini1995]. Rule-based indicators (C3, C5, L3, L4, O2) use rule checks with proportion charts. These references establish that each method exists. Their fit to each use is not yet confirmed at claim level.

## 7. Matched false-alarm calibration

A shorter TAFD only counts if the systems raise false alarms at the same rate.

1. Generate two disjoint sets of null streams (SC00): 1,000 for calibration and 1,000 for evaluation, with separate seeds. Each stream has 104 monitored windows.
2. A **false alarm** is the start of an alert episode in a null stream (or before s0 in a perturbed stream). The rate is episodes per 1,000 monitored windows.
3. Set the margins m_Y from the calibration streams so that each system's yellow false-alarm rate equals the budget f* (D2). For CCA under the union rule this is a common scaling factor applied to all indicator margins, and under the primary component split it is the per-component calibration described in section 6. Orange and red margins use fixed multiples of m_Y (2 and 4 as placeholders). Every arm and every routing configuration is calibrated separately to the same f*.
4. Verify on the evaluation null streams that each system meets f* within Monte Carlo error. Only then run the perturbed streams.
5. Report results as a curve over f* of 1, 5, and 20 per 1,000 windows, not only at one budget.
6. Repeat steps 1 to 4 for every configuration in section 3, because window size and label lag change null behavior. That is 5 configurations with 2,000 null streams each, 10,000 in total.

Calibrating on one null set and evaluating on another prevents optimistic false-alarm estimates.

## 8. Replication and precision

The primary configuration uses R = 500 replicates per cell, with all systems run on the same stream (common random numbers) so comparisons are paired. The standard error of an estimated detection proportion at p = 0.5 is 0.022, and its 95 percent interval half-width is about 0.044. Paired differences have their own variance, which depends on the correlation between systems.

| Component | Cells | Replicates | Streams |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary configuration | 105 | 500 | 52,500 |
| One-factor sensitivity (2 window sizes, 2 label lags, subgroup share) | 429 | 200 | 85,800 |
| Null streams (5 configurations, calibration plus evaluation) | not applicable | 2,000 each | 10,000 |
| RH3 mixture streams (section 9) | not applicable | 2,000 | 2,000 |
| Total | | | 150,300 |

R is fixed in advance. If precision is insufficient, a uniform increase across all cells is the only allowed change, and it is decided before any analysis is unblinded. Choosing extra replicates only for cells where a decision is close would be data-dependent and is not permitted. Compute cost has not been estimated. Milestone M3 measures the per-window cost of the monitors, and the grid is not committed until that number is known.

## 9. Estimands and analysis

For each cell, on the paired streams:

- Kaplan-Meier curves of time to detection [kaplan1958].
- Restricted mean TAFD (RMST) up to h = 40 [royston2013]. The primary estimand (decision D19) is the reduction of system B against system A in windows, RMST_A - RMST_B, with a paired bootstrap interval. The relative reduction rho = (RMST_A - RMST_B) / RMST_A is secondary, because it is unstable when TAFD is about 2 windows, and it is bounded by the censoring rate because never-detected replicates enter at h. For anticipatory detections the lead time (onset minus detection) is reported separately.
- Hazard ratio for detection from a Cox model [cox1972] stratified by scenario, with variance robust to clustering by stream, as a secondary summary.
- Proportion detected by h, false-alarm rate, missed-risk events, and alert burden.

Confidence intervals come from a percentile bootstrap that resamples whole streams, so both systems stay paired.

The same four tests at lags 0 and 8 are adjusted separately and reported as sensitivity. Sub-threshold cells are excluded. ### 9.2 Attribution analysis

For each scenario, report how often each indicator fires at or above yellow before the horizon. Primary responders should fire often, and `expected_null` indicators should fire only at the nominal rate. Departures show false attribution or unmodeled coupling.

## 10. EHOR estimator study

Because the simulation knows which outputs are errors and which were intercepted, it can evaluate the design-weighted EHOR estimator against truth. The comparator is a naive estimator whose denominator is the errors that reviewers flagged plus errors discovered later through outcome reports. It is the estimator a health system would compute without an independent audit. The study varies the audit sampling fraction (1, 5, and 20 percent) and stratification, and reports bias, root-mean-square error, and confidence-interval coverage. Weighting uses Horvitz-Thompson estimation [horvitz1952]. It tests the claim in `Instrument-Specification.md` section 7.2 that counting only visible errors inflates EHOR, in scenarios SC10 and SC11. The identity EHOR = Cov_R x EHOR_c is checked as a unit test, because it holds algebraically.

## 11. Reviewer and workflow models

These are assumptions, not measurements.

**Reviewer.** Review coverage, sensitivity to an AI error as a logistic function of model confidence and severity, an automation-bias parameter (probability of accepting an output whatever its quality), a fatigue term that grows with workload, and a lognormal review latency. Each parameter is sampled from a grid, and the results are reported across the grid so no single assumed reviewer drives a conclusion. If reader-study data become available, the grid should be anchored to it.

**Implementation status (2026-09-20).** Implemented: coverage, a logistic recognition probability in confidence and severity, automation bias, a false-rejection rate, and lognormal latency against a deadline (the point of no return). Not implemented: the fatigue term, and the grid. The workflow model (section 16.8) implements delivery probability, lognormal turnaround against a service limit, a time-critical share, workflow completion, alert duplication, and downstream verification. It does not implement an event queue with arrivals, escalation acknowledgment, an action log with fatigue, or reporting lag and under-reporting of incidents. The EHOR estimator study and the reviewer-failure pilot (sections 16.2 and 16.4) use one assumed reviewer, so the grid this section promises is still owed for them. For RH3 a one-at-a-time sweep is in section 16.6, and the full grid is still owed.

**Workflow.** An event queue with arrivals, review latency, escalation delivery probability, alert duplication, and infrastructure latency. A share of cases is flagged time-critical, and delay matters only for those.

## 14. Threats to validity

- Injected perturbations are simplified and may miss the structure of real drift. Tier 2 reduces this and does not remove it.
- D2 to D8 are judgments. Results should be shown across their sensitivity ranges, and a conclusion that flips inside a plausible range should be reported as fragile.
- The reviewer and workflow models are assumptions. Conclusions for RH3 and RH4 are conditional on them, and for the reason in section 9.1 they demonstrate detectability, not prevalence of the failures.
- Onset is defined from a harm threshold. A different definition can shift TAFD, so the sensitivity analysis over kappa is part of the primary reporting, and sub-threshold cells are counted.
- Tier 2 replicates are resamples of one dataset, so their intervals understate variation across sites and years.
- A natural between-site shift has no known onset and is confounded by differences in case mix and practice between hospitals. Its harm-defined onset would have to be estimated from noisy real labels, so results from it are exploratory.
- The 34 indicators are not independent, and correlated indicators can inflate agreement in the attribution analysis.
- Only predictive AI is covered. No result here supports claims about generative or agentic systems.
- Positive results would show detection under simulation. They would not show patient benefit.

**Findings that bear on the design**

1. **M1 is met, with a tight sensitivity band.** Sensitivity ranged 0.7262 to 0.7795 against a band of 0.72 to 0.78, so the upper edge was met by 0.0005. The bands are unchanged, and a different seed set could fail the sensitivity band.
2. **Harm model (D10).** Counting false positives raised baseline RHE from 19 to 30, which dilutes relative changes, so sub-threshold cells rose from 14 of 21 to 17 of 24 at kappa = 0.25. With the pre-stated rule (the high level must reach 1.25 at full effect), SC03 and SC05 became `primary_tafd: false`. Covariate shift is now above threshold at its high level (ratio 1.28).
3. **Calibration and harm cannot both be had with stable AUROC (D9).** A world change mild enough to keep AUROC near baseline gives a harm ratio of only 1.03 to 1.10. One strong enough to harm (about 1.30 at slope 0.6) lowers AUROC by about 0.095. RH2 evidence therefore comes from SC08 and SC09.
4. **Matched calibration works, and it has a price.** At 5 alert episodes per 1,000 windows the conventional monitor needs h = 5.3 and the 12-indicator CCA set needs h = 9.4. The common-scaling union rule is a multiplicity penalty.
5. **Pilot detection results (exploratory, 150 replicates per cell, budget 5 per 1,000 windows, label lags 0, 2, and 8).** Reduction in mean TAFD, in windows, of CCA (component split) against conventional monitoring, for the high abrupt cells. Positive means CCA detects earlier:

### 16.2 Reviewer layer, first step: EHOR estimator study (M6, first pass, exploratory)

Built test-first (13 tests, 11 mutants killed, one equivalent): `automation_bias` and `false_reject` reviewer parameters (defaults 0 and 0.02, which leave every earlier result unchanged), a per-case review sampler (`src/sim_module/review.py`), and the truth, Horvitz-Thompson, and naive EHOR estimators with a linearized standard error and a confidence-stratified Poisson audit (`src/eval_module/ehor.py`). `scripts/m6_ehor_study.py` wrote `M6-EHOR-Study.md` (300 replicates of 20,000 cases per condition, SC10 and SC11 at every level).

- **The audit estimator is unbiased.** Bias is within 0.016 in every condition (the largest are at a 1 percent audit). The identity EHOR = Cov_R x EHOR_c holds exactly.
- **The naive comparator is inflated, and more so as oversight degrades**: +0.05 at baseline, +0.25 in SC10 high, +0.29 in SC11 high. The size depends on the assumed 30 percent discovery of missed errors, which is a placeholder. The direction does not.
- **Precision needs a large audit.** At a 1 percent audit the interval covers 0.57 to 0.93 of the time and the RMSE (0.08 to 0.15) exceeds the naive bias at baseline. At 5 percent, coverage is 0.83 to 0.98. At 20 percent it is 0.91 to 0.98. A small audit is not worth it when oversight is near baseline. It pays when oversight has degraded.
- **Confidence stratification did not help reliably.** Near baseline its RMSE was 12 to 26 percent worse than the flat design at 5 and 20 percent audits. Under degraded oversight the two were about equal (stratified better in some SC10 cells, worse in others), and its coverage was lower at small fractions. Errors are not concentrated enough in the low-confidence stratum in this simulator to repay the unequal weights. The instrument specification's suggestion to oversample likely errors should be worded as optional until Tier 2 shows otherwise.
- The IIR estimator is unbiased (worst bias 0.0007).

The reviewer layer is built (sections 16.2, 16.4, and 16.5) and so is the workflow layer (section 16.8). SC12 and SC15 have schedules but no study yet.

### 16.20 D25 accepted: RH4 counts detection within 4 windows (2026-09-21)

**Review.** The ceiling argument is structural: with alert episodes at a budget of B per 1,000 windows, the model-level arm alone detects a share 1 - exp(-B x h / 1,000) of replicates within h windows of onset, so theta4 cannot exceed exp(-B x h / 1,000) apart from sampling noise (the 40-window estimate at lag 0, +0.306, is 0.006 above 0.30). At the primary budget of 30 that is 0.30 for h = 40, 0.89 for h = 4, 0.94 for h = 2, and 0.79 for h = 8. The observed model-level detection rates (64 to 75 percent within 40 windows) match the formula. The horizon of 4 windows is about one to two governance cycles (2 windows at 500 patients a week, 1 at 250). It was chosen after the results were seen, so it is disclosed as such, and the sensitivities 2, 8, and 40 are reported.
